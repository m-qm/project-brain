# How to answer a review, an email, and a quote from the same notes

The review, the email, and the quote can come from three different drafts. Each one can fill a gap with a price, a client, or a date nobody wrote. Then the client asks you to honor it.

## What can come from the same notes

The notes say what you sell, who it is for, and what stays `[CONFIRM]`. From that you can prepare:

1. A calm Google review, using lines you already approved. A one-star review, a refund, or a price that is not written waits for your yes.
2. An email or a form message, using those same lines. A request the notes cannot answer waits.
3. A LinkedIn text, once the article is published.
4. A short quote, or the brief you take to a call. A missing price stays marked.
5. A stop before you send. If the draft names a price the notes do not contain, it does not go out.

## The review example

On the home page, the calm review uses the name Ana: "Clear weekly goals. Thank you."
The draft says: "Thank you, Ana. From the folder: what you sell and who it is for."

The one-star review uses the name Luis: "One star. I want a refund."
It is not posted. The suggestion is: "Thank you, Luis. I will reply myself." You get a notice.

Those two names are the ones on the home page drawing. They are not a published client.

The price gap is in the other article. The site said on request. The draft wrote "from €99." That line stays as Price: `[CONFIRM]`.

## What you still do

You read the draft and say yes before it goes out. A new fact enters the folder through the wizard, the page where you paste your site. The next draft can use that line. Saying yes to a review does not change the folder.

## How the folder is read

Agent KBase keeps the notes in git. Git is the history of each change. Mastra is the program that chains the reading and the draft. The steps are in [Chain the draft](/learn/13-mastra).

## Who it is for

A business with a site and no marketing team. A shop, a studio, or a small consultancy.
