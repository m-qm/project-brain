# How to stop a draft asking for your personal phone

If you run a shop or a studio, the last lines of a post are the ask. A bad ask is not a weak call to action. It is a draft that wants your invoices, a password, or your personal mobile.

A shop owner is searching how to publish without handing a stranger the keys to the business. That is buying intent. This answers that search.

## The problem (yours, not the builder’s)

A realistic example: the draft closes with “WhatsApp me on my personal mobile and send last month’s invoices so we can start”. You did not mean to publish your phone. You did not mean to collect billing files in a blog post. A reader saves the number. The post is now a request you have to honour or delete in public.

The model is trying to be helpful at the end. Helpful is not the same as safe.

## What is not enough

“Make the ending stronger” is not enough. A generic “DM me” is not enough if the destination is a personal line. An auto-publish tool will ship that close on every post after this one.

## What works: the ask is the list, the site, or the brand line

Before the text goes live, someone marks this yes or rewrites the close:

The ask is the waitlist, the site, or the brand WhatsApp. Never invoices, passwords, or a personal phone.

The other four checks sit on the same list. This post is only this one. The full list is public: [agentkbase.com/company-brain](/company-brain).

## How we run it here

Agent KBase does not collect invoices, passwords, or a personal phone for this loop. The article worker leaves the draft pending. The close of this file is the check: the waitlist on the site. A human still has to say yes before it is a page.

## Who this is for

A shop, a studio, or a one or two person consultancy with a website and no marketing team. Not a department that already has an agency.
