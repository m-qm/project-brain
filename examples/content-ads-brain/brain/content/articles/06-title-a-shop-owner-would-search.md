# How to title a post a shop owner would actually search

If you run a shop or a studio, the title is the search. A jargon word in the headline means the person who pays you never opens the post. They were never looking for that word.

They are searching the problem on the floor: a post that does not match what the shop sells, a week with nothing published, a price the site does not list. That is buying intent. This answers that search.

## The problem (yours, not the builder’s)

A realistic example: the draft is titled “What RAG means for your content stack”. Nobody who runs the studio searches that on a Tuesday. The same draft, retitled around the missed week or the price that is not on the site, is a post they would open.

The model titles what it knows. You need the title to be the problem you already have.

## What is not enough

A clever headline is not enough. A keyword tool is not enough if the keyword is a builder word. Publishing the jargon title “so the blog looks technical” trains the next draft to do it again.

## What works: a shop or studio problem in the title

Before the text goes live, someone marks this yes or rewrites the title:

The title is a shop or studio problem, not a jargon word. If the title only makes sense to someone who builds agents, it is cut.

A shop, a studio, or a one or two person consultancy with a website and no marketing team. Not a department that already has an agency.
