# How to see where each line in a draft came from

If you run a shop or a studio, a one-minute review only works when each fact points at a page. A price, a pack, or a deadline with no source is a line you cannot mark yes. You would be guessing.

A shop owner is searching how to check an AI draft before it goes on the site. That is buying intent. This answers that search.

## The problem (yours, not the builder’s)

A realistic example: the draft says a pack includes a two-week delivery. Your services page says “on request”. The draft does not say which sentence came from the site and which sentence the model added. You can feel that something is off. You cannot point at the line and cut it in a minute, so the review becomes a meeting, or you publish and hope.

Fluency hides the gap. A source line does not.

## What is not enough

Trusting the draft because it “sounds like you” is not enough. A plagiarism check is not enough: the risk here is an invented offer, not a copied paragraph. Asking the model to “cite sources” after it has already published is too late.

## What works: your site or a public page, named on the draft

Before the text goes live, someone marks this yes or sends it back:

The draft says where each fact came from: your site or a public page. If a line has no source, it is cut or marked for you to confirm. It does not go live in between.

The other four checks sit on the same list. This post is only this one. The full list is public: [agentkbase.com/company-brain](/company-brain).

## How we run it here

Agent KBase keeps the offer in git. The article worker writes the draft and lists the brain files it used. A human can open those files and the public page and compare. This draft lists them below. It stays pending until that comparison is yes.

## Who this is for

A shop, a studio, or a one or two person consultancy with a website and no marketing team. Not a department that already has an agency.
